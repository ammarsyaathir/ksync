  5 [-] This chapter introduces K-Sync Malaysia, a web-based platform proposed to centralize K-pop event information and provide an organized space for fan community discussions. It explains the background of the study, current business process, problem statements, proposed solutions, project aim, objectives, scope, significance and organization of the thesis. The chapter focuses on three needs identified through literature review and preliminary data collection: making K-pop events easier to discover, organizing event-related discussions, and improving the transparency of event verification and information management.
  6 [Heading1 [list]] Background of Study
  8 <empty>
  8 [-] Korean popular music, commonly known as K-pop, has developed into an international entertainment culture that involves music, performances, online communities and fan activities. Fans do not only listen to songs; they also follow artist updates, attend concerts and fan meetings, participate in cupsleeve events, support fan projects and communicate with other fans. These activities depend on the exchange of information before, during and after an event. For this reason, access to event announcements and opportunities to discuss event-related matters are important parts of the fan experience.
 10 <empty>
 10 [-] Social media platforms play an important role in how K-pop fans discover events and interact with other fans. X, Instagram, TikTok, Telegram and organizer websites are among the channels used to share posters, schedules, venue information and announcements. Research on Malaysian K-pop fandom has found that social media supports fan engagement and community participation (Roslan et al., 2024; Roslan et al., 2025). Recent work also describes how fans voluntarily create, seek and share fandom-related information through social platforms (Roslan et al., 2026). These studies provide a basis for understanding why online spaces are important for event discovery and fan communication.
 12 <empty>
 12 [-] However, event information may be distributed across different accounts, posts and online communities rather than presented in one consistent listing. An organizer may publish the main announcement on one platform and post additional details or corrections elsewhere. Fans therefore may need to search several sources to find the date, time, venue, requirements and latest updates. If they need further clarification, they may also ask questions in group chats or search earlier conversations. Important messages can become difficult to retrieve when many unrelated messages appear, and questions may be repeated when previous answers cannot easily be found.
 14 <empty>
 14 [-] A further concern is how fans determine whether an event announcement is reliable and up to date. A post may be shared by an unofficial account or reposted without the original source, while earlier details may remain visible after an update. Research by Geels et al. (2024) shows that source-related signals on social media do not automatically guarantee that readers will make accurate judgements about the credibility of a message. This supports the need to consider the evidence behind an event announcement rather than treating an online post or visible badge alone as conclusive proof. In a centralized event platform, a defined review process can help make the basis for publishing information more consistent and understandable.
 16 <empty>
 16 [-] The current business process generally requires organizers or fan communities to publish event announcements through their existing channels. Fans then discover the announcements, compare information across sources and use community chats to ask questions or share information. When a detail is missing, unclear or difficult to retrieve, the fan may need to search again or ask the same question in another group. The following flowchart summarizes this process and highlights the points where event discovery, discussion and information checking can become difficult.

 19 [-] As illustrated in Figure 1.1, the current process depends on fans searching across separate platforms and revisiting community conversations to locate event details. The process does not necessarily mean that every event announcement is inaccurate or that all discussions are difficult to follow. Instead, it shows the practical challenges that may occur when information is spread across multiple channels and is not organized around a single event listing and discussion space.
 20 [-] To address these issues, K-Sync Malaysia is proposed as a centralized web-based platform for K-pop event information and fan community discussions in Malaysia. Fans will be able to browse and search event listings, participate in event-specific discussions and use a community space to ask questions or share information. Organizers will submit event details through a public form without creating an account, while a system administrator will review submitted information before approved listings are published. Standardized review criteria and records of decisions will help improve transparency in the platformΓÇÖs event verification process.
 21 [-] Key terms. In this project, an event listing refers to a structured page containing information about a K-pop event. A fan community discussion refers to user-generated posts and replies related to an event or general K-pop topics. Event verification refers to the administratorΓÇÖs review of submitted event details and supporting sources against the platformΓÇÖs criteria; it does not guarantee event quality or safety.
 22 [Heading1 [list]] Problem Statements
 24 <empty>
 24 [-] The problems addressed by this project were identified from the current event-information process, a questionnaire completed by 30 respondents, and interviews with representatives from Malaysian K-pop fan communities. The questionnaire examined how respondents discover K-pop events and the difficulties they experience when discussing events across group chats or platforms. Interviews with the MKF Malaysia Instagram administrator and Ms Syazwani from Cupsleeve Malaysia provided additional context about how event information is published and shared. Three problems are discussed below: fragmented event discovery, disorganized community discussions, and the need for a consistent and transparent event verification process. The questionnaire sample is limited and is used as evidence about the respondents who participated, not as a national estimate of all K-pop fans in Malaysia.
 25 [Heading2 [list]] Difficulty Discovering K-pop Event Information Across Multiple Platforms
 26 [-] K-pop event information in Malaysia is published through different online platforms, including X, TikTok, Instagram, Telegram and official organizer websites. Each platform may contain different event announcements, and the information may not be presented in a consistent format. Fans who want to discover concerts, fan meetings, cupsleeve events or other fan activities may have to follow multiple accounts and compare separate posts to locate event details. This can make event discovery less convenient, especially when fans are interested in several artists or events occurring within a similar period.
 28 <empty>
 28 [-] Research concerning Malaysian K-pop fans shows that social media is used for fan activities, connection and information sharing. Roslan et al. (2024) discussed how facilitating conditions influence social media use among K-pop fans, while Roslan et al. (2025) examined user experience and effort expectancy in fan-related social media use. These studies support the importance of social media in fandom but do not, by themselves, measure how difficult Malaysian fans find event discovery. The questionnaire for this project therefore provides local evidence about the platforms used by its respondents.
 30 <empty>
 30 [-] Among the 30 questionnaire respondents, 83.3% reported using X (formerly Twitter) to find K-pop event information, while 56.7% used TikTok. Telegram channels and official event organizer websites were each reported by 40.0% of respondents; 36.7% used Instagram and 16.7% received information through friends or word of mouth. These responses show that participants use several different channels to discover event information. In addition, 70% of respondents reported that they sometimes or very often missed event information because posts appeared too late in social media feeds. During an interview on 26 April 2026, the MKF Malaysia Instagram administrator explained that the timing and distribution of social media posts can affect when followers see updates (MKF Malaysia Instagram administrator, personal communication, April 26, 2026). This interview provides context for the survey findings, although it does not establish that every fan experiences delayed updates.
 32 <empty>
 32 [-] The findings indicate that fans in this sample rely on multiple sources to discover K-pop events and that some respondents experience delays in seeing announcements. Searching across separate platforms can require additional effort and makes it harder to obtain a consolidated view of upcoming events. A centralized listing with search and filtering functions is therefore proposed to help fans discover relevant events and view the available information in a consistent format.

 36 [Heading2 [list]] Disorganized Community Discussions and Difficulty Retrieving Event-Related Information
 38 <empty>
 38 [-] K-pop fans use group chats and online communities to discuss events, ask questions, share updates and communicate with other fans. However, when conversations take place across several groups or platforms, useful information may become difficult to retrieve. Important messages can be buried under newer messages, repeated questions may appear when earlier answers cannot be found, and event information may be scattered across different group conversations. As a result, fans may need to scroll through long chat histories or switch between platforms to locate a previous answer or an event update.
 40 <empty>
 40 [-] Research on K-pop fan communities recognizes that social media supports interaction, participation and the sharing of fandom-related information. Roslan et al. (2026) describe how fans actively seek, organize and share information through online fan spaces. These findings demonstrate the role of community information sharing, but they do not establish that every K-pop group chat is disorganized. The problem in this project is supported more directly by the questionnaire questions designed to collect respondentsΓÇÖ experiences of event-related discussions.
 42 <empty>
 42 [-] Question 24 asked respondents what difficulties they experience when discussing K-pop events across different group chats or platforms. The responses shared for this project include event information being scattered across different groups, difficulty finding previous discussions or shared information, repeated questions, and important messages being buried among other messages. These themes describe retrieval and organization problems rather than simply a lack of event announcements. Question 25 then asked respondents to rate the usefulness of a centralized K-pop community space where fans could discuss events, ask questions and share information without switching between platforms. Question 26 asked which community features respondents would most like to use, including event information sharing, event discussion forums, a general K-pop forum, questions and answers, and shared-interest group discussions.
 43 [-] The responses to Questions 24ΓÇô26 provide a direct reason to include community discussions as part of K-Sync Malaysia. However, the response rows available for drafting this chapter do not include the complete distribution for all 30 respondents, so no overall percentage or average rating is claimed here. The final thesis should include the full frequency or percentage summary for these questions once it has been calculated from the complete questionnaire dataset. Based on the recorded response themes and the features presented in Question 26, an event-based discussion space with searchable questions and answers and a separate general K-pop discussion area is proposed.
 44 [-] A centralized community feature could help organize conversations according to an event or topic, making it easier for fans to return to earlier discussions and share useful information in a relevant place. This feature would not eliminate all repeated questions or replace every external group chat. Instead, it would provide an additional organized space where fans can search event-related conversations and find previously shared answers, reducing the need to switch between several platforms for every discussion.

 48 [Heading2 [list]] Need for a Consistent and Transparent Event Verification Process
 50 <empty>
 50 [-] Finding an event announcement and discussing it with other fans do not automatically establish that the information is official or current. Posts may be shared by accounts other than the original organizer, and reposted information may not include a direct link to the original announcement. Event details may also be changed after the first post is published. When fans obtain information from several sources, they may need to decide which source to trust or return to the organizerΓÇÖs page to confirm the latest details. This creates a need for the proposed centralized platform to explain how event listings are checked before they are presented as verified.
 52 <empty>
 52 [-] Geels et al. (2024) examined how source identity and credential information affect perceived social media credibility. Their experiments found that visible source signals do not always lead users to accurate credibility judgments, and that source authenticity can be confused with the credibility of a particular message. Therefore, a verification label alone should not be treated as proof that every detail of an event is correct. A platform should consider the underlying event evidence and clearly communicate the scope of its verification process. Research on online content moderation also identifies consistency and clarity of decision criteria as important challenges for platforms that review user-generated content (Schaffner et al., 2024).
 54 <empty>
 54 [-] In the current cross-platform process described in this study, fans may need to check event details through the original organizer or other sources themselves. The survey demonstrates that respondents use multiple platforms to locate event information, but it does not directly prove that fake events are common or that existing administrators are biased. Similarly, the interviews provide context about event information sharing but do not establish deliberate misconduct. The problem addressed here is the need for K-Sync Malaysia to have its own defined process for reviewing public event submissions, applying criteria consistently and recording the basis for publication decisions.
 56 <empty>
 56 [-] Without a defined workflow, event submissions may be reviewed inconsistently, and rejected organizers may not understand why their submissions were not published. A documented decision process can improve accountability by recording whether an event was approved or rejected and the reason for that decision. Suzor et al. (2019) argue that meaningful transparency in content moderation involves giving affected users clear information about decisions and the rules applied. For K-Sync Malaysia, this supports the inclusion of standardized verification criteria, recorded rejection reasons, administrative audit logs and a mechanism for fans to report incorrect or outdated listings. These features are intended to reduce avoidable inconsistency and improve traceability; they cannot guarantee that all decisions will be free from bias or that every event will remain accurate after publication.
 57 [Heading1 [list]] Proposed Solutions
 58 [ListParagraph] 
 59 [-] To address the problems identified in Section 1.2, this project proposes K-Sync Malaysia: A Centralized Platform for K-Pop Event Information and Fan Community Discussions. The system will provide three main solutions: centralized event listings, an organized fan community discussion space, and a standardized event submission and verification process. Each solution is mapped to one problem so that the scope of development remains aligned with the evidence gathered during requirement analysis.
 60 [Heading2 [list]] Centralized K-pop Event Information Platform
 61 [ListParagraph] 
 62 [-] The first proposed solution is to develop a centralized event information platform where fans can discover K-pop events in Malaysia through organized listings. The system will provide event pages for activities such as concerts, fan meetings, cupsleeve events, exhibitions and pop-up events. Fans will be able to browse event listings and search or filter them according to available criteria, such as event name, artist or group, date, location and event category. Each event page will present the available details in a consistent structure, including the event date, time, venue, description and links to relevant source announcements. This design aims to make the essential information easier to locate without requiring fans to search through multiple timelines individually.
 64 <empty>
 64 [-] This solution directly addresses the difficulty of discovering event information across different platforms. By organizing approved event details in one place, K-Sync Malaysia will give fans a central point from which to explore upcoming activities and revisit the information they need. The system will not replace organizersΓÇÖ original announcements or control how external platforms distribute posts. Instead, it will provide a structured directory with search and filtering functions, thereby reducing the effort needed to discover events and compare their publicly available details.
 66 <empty>
 66 [Heading2 [list]] Event-Based Community Discussion and Information Sharing
 67 [ListParagraph] 
 68 [-] The second proposed solution is to provide an organized fan community space within K-Sync Malaysia. The community will include an event-specific discussion area where registered fans can ask questions, reply to other fans and share useful information related to a listed event. A general K-pop discussion area may also be provided for conversations that are not limited to one event. Questions and answers, topic-based discussions and event information sharing will help structure conversations so that users can return to information relevant to a particular topic or event. Community participation will require a fan account, while the system administrator will be responsible for reviewing reported content and enforcing the platformΓÇÖs discussion rules.
 70 <empty>
 70 [-] This solution responds directly to the issues identified in Question 24, including information being scattered across different group chats, important messages being buried, repeated questions and difficulty locating previous discussions. Organizing discussions by event and providing searchable topics or question-and-answer content can make useful information easier to retrieve. The community feature will serve as an additional organized space rather than a replacement for every external platform. It is intended to reduce unnecessary switching between groups, support information sharing and make event-related conversations easier for fans to follow.
 72 <empty>
 72 [Heading2 [list]] Standardized Event Submission and Verification
 73 [ListParagraph] 
 74 [-] The third proposed solution is to develop a standardized event submission and verification workflow. An event organizer will submit an event through a public form without creating a registered account. The form will collect the event name, date, time, location, description, organizer contact details and supporting evidence, such as an official announcement URL or relevant social media account. Submitted events will be marked as pending until the system administrator reviews the details and checks the available evidence against the platformΓÇÖs verification criteria. The administrator will then approve the submission for publication or reject it with a recorded reason. Only approved submissions will appear as verified listings in the public event directory.
 76 <empty>
 76 [-] This solution addresses the need for a consistent and transparent way to manage submitted event information. A standard checklist will help the administrator review submissions against the same basic requirements, while recorded decisions and rejection reasons will make the process easier to trace. An audit log will record important administrative actions, and fans will be able to report listings that appear incorrect or outdated. These measures aim to improve the reliability and accountability of the platformΓÇÖs information management process. The verification status will mean that a submission has been reviewed against K-Sync MalaysiaΓÇÖs criteria; it will not guarantee event safety, quality or the organizerΓÇÖs future conduct.

 92 [Heading1 [list]] Project Scope
 93 [ListParagraph] 
 94 [-] The project scope defines the functions, users, geographical coverage, study participants, technologies and time period covered by the development of K-Sync Malaysia. The system is limited to event information, fan community discussion and event verification. It does not provide ticket booking, online payments, merchandise purchasing or real-time merchandise stock tracking.
 95 [Heading2] 1.6.1 Functional Scope
 96 [-] The system supports three types of interaction: fans with registered accounts, event organizers who submit information through a public form, and the system administrator. Event organizers do not need to create an account merely to submit an event. The main system functions are as follows:
 97 [ListBullet] Fan account functions: register, log in, log out and manage profile information.
 98 [ListBullet] Event discovery: browse published events, search by keywords, filter event listings and view event details and available source links.
 99 [ListBullet] Fan community: participate in event-based discussions, contribute to general K-pop topics, ask and answer questions, share useful event information and report inappropriate or incorrect content.
100 [ListBullet] Public event submission: allow an organizer to enter event details and provide supporting evidence without registering an account.
101 [ListBullet] Administrator functions: review pending submissions, check supporting information, approve or reject submissions, record rejection reasons, manage published event information, review reports and access administrative audit records.
102 [-] The system will not include a separate organizer dashboard, organizer account management, ticket sales, event reservations, payment integration, fan support merchandise ordering or live stock tracking. These exclusions keep the system focused on event discovery, information sharing and verification. Community moderation will be handled by the system administrator rather than by a separate fanclub administrator role in this project version.
103 [Heading2] 1.6.2 Temporal Scope
104 [-] The project covers two academic semesters. Semester 5, from March 2026 to August 2026, focuses on proposal preparation, requirement gathering, literature review and system design. Early development is planned during the semester break from August 2026 to October 2026. Semester 6, from October 2026 to February 2027, focuses on completing the system implementation and preparing the final project deliverables. Long-term maintenance after the academic project period is outside the main scope. Any functional checks or evaluation activities will be limited to those required by the project plan and available time.
105 [Heading2] 1.6.3 Geographical Scope
106 [-] K-Sync Malaysia is intended for K-pop events held in Malaysia. Event categories may include concerts, fan meetings, cupsleeve events, exhibitions, pop-up events and other relevant fan activities. The platform will present event information with identifiable locations in Malaysia. It is not intended to operate as a worldwide event directory, and the project will not provide location-based ticket purchasing or real-time GPS tracking.
107 [Heading2] 1.6.4 Sampling Scope
108 [-] The requirement-gathering activities include a questionnaire involving 30 K-pop fans and interviews with representatives of Malaysian K-pop fan communities. The questionnaire collects information about event discovery, community discussion difficulties, perceived usefulness of a centralized community space and preferred community features. Interviews with the MKF Malaysia Instagram administrator and Ms Syazwani from Cupsleeve Malaysia provide contextual information about event announcements and information sharing. The sample size is limited, and the results should not be generalized to all K-pop fans in Malaysia. For Questions 24ΓÇô26, the final thesis should report the complete response counts or percentages after the full dataset has been checked.
109 [Heading2] 1.6.5 Technological Scope
110 [-] K-Sync Malaysia will be implemented as a responsive web-based application that can be accessed through desktop and mobile web browsers. HTML, CSS and JavaScript will be used for the interface, Node.js with Hono will handle server-side routes, and Supabase PostgreSQL will store relational data such as accounts, event listings, submissions, discussion posts and reports. Cloudflare-compatible deployment is planned for the web application. The project does not include separate native Android or iOS applications.
111 [Heading2] 1.6.6 Limitations
112 [-] The project has several limitations. First, the questionnaire includes only 30 respondents, so the findings may not represent all Malaysian K-pop fans. Second, the system depends on organizers submitting accurate event details and sufficient supporting evidence; administrator review cannot guarantee that every event will remain accurate after publication. Third, event updates may be published externally after a K-Sync listing has been approved, so the platform requires the information to be updated when new details are reported or identified. Fourth, a single system administrator may still make mistakes or exercise bias despite standard criteria and audit logs. Finally, the development period limits the number of advanced functions and the duration of evaluation. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the project outcomes.
